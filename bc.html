📚 What is Business Continuity?

Business Continuity (BC) is the coordinated process of ensuring uninterrupted cloud services to consumers by implementing automated procedures to handle failures, disasters, and outages.

Core Definition & Purpose

Business continuity layer operates at the top of the cloud architecture, overlaying all other layers to provide reliability and fault tolerance.

Key Characteristics:
  • Automated Processes: BC operations run automatically through orchestration - e.g., if a service requires VM backup every 6 hours, it happens automatically every 6 hours without manual intervention
  • Three Core Activities:
    • Preparing for a system outage (proactive planning)
    • Responding to a system outage (immediate reaction)
    • Recovering from a system outage (restoration)
  • Mission-Critical Protection: Prevents interruption of critical services that businesses depend on
  • Service Level Agreement (SLA) Compliance: Meets agreed availability requirements with customers
  • Rapid Recovery: Reestablishes impacted services as swiftly and smoothly as possible using automated processes

Two Approaches to BC

Approach Description Purpose Proactive Measures Business impact analysis, risk assessment, building resilient infrastructure, deploying data protection solutions (backup & replication) Prevent outages before they happen Reactive Countermeasures Disaster recovery plans to be invoked when service failure occurs Minimize downtime after failure occurs

🏗️ Cloud Architecture & BC Layer Position

The Business Continuity layer sits at the top of the cloud architecture stack and provides protection across all underlying layers:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ BC Layer │ ← Overlays entire stack │ (Availability, Failover, Backup, Resilience) │ ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ Service Layer │ │ (Applications, APIs, Databases) │ ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ Service Orchestration Layer │ │ (Automation, Scheduling, Management) │ ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ Control Layer │ │ (VM Management, Network Control) │ ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ Virtual Layer │ │ (Virtual Machines, Virtual Networks) │ ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ Physical Layer │ │ (Hardware Compute, Storage, Networking) │ └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Dependency Chain

The BC layer depends on ALL lower layers. Cloud service availability relies on:

  • Infrastructure reliability: Physical components must be redundant and fault-tolerant
  • Virtual layer stability: VMs and virtual networks must support high availability
  • Control layer automation: Systems must automatically manage and redistribute workloads
  • Orchestration layer: Services must be deployable and manageable across multiple zones/locations

📖 Essential Definitions & Metrics

Availability Metrics

Term Definition Impact
Uptime Time between two outages (scheduled or unscheduled) - when service is RUNNING Higher is better; goal is maximize uptime
Downtime Time elapsed DURING an outage - when service is UNAVAILABLE Lower is better; goal is minimize downtime
Service Availability (%) Percentage of agreed service time that service is actually available Direct measure of BC effectiveness; determines SLA compliance

Service Availability Calculation

Service Availability (%) = (Agreed Service Time - Downtime) / Agreed Service Time × 100%
Example Calculation:
Service offered: Monday-Friday, 8am-6pm (10 hours/day × 5 days = 50 hours/week)
Downtime during week: 3 hours

Service Availability = (50 - 3) / 50 × 100 = 47 / 50 × 100 = 94%
📌 Exam Tip: You must be able to calculate availability percentages. Practice with different scenarios: 1 hour downtime, 6 hours downtime, 24-hour operations vs business hours only.

Recovery Metrics: RPO vs RTO

⚠️ CRITICAL EXAM DIFFERENCE - DO NOT CONFUSE THESE TWO

Metric Full Name Meaning Example
RPO Recovery Point Objective TIME BETWEEN BACKUPS - maximum acceptable data loss If RPO=6 hours, backups occur every 6 hours. Can lose up to 6 hours of data.
RTO Recovery Time Objective TIME TO RESTORE SERVICE - maximum acceptable downtime If RTO=2 hours, service must be restored within 2 hours of failure
Memory Device: RPO = Restore POINT (when the last backup happened), RTO = Restore TIME (how fast we recover)

Disaster Recovery (DR)

Disaster Recovery (DR): The coordinated process of restoring IT infrastructure and data required to support ongoing cloud services after a natural or human-induced disaster occurs (earthquake, fire, cyberattack, etc.).

Key Concept: Requires maintaining a secondary data center (DR site) at a pre-planned level of operational readiness. When the primary data center fails, the DR site automatically takes over.

Dependency & Failover

Service Dependency: A service is dependent if it relies on another service or component. If the dependent service/component fails, the primary service also fails.

Example: A web application running on VM-A depends on database server running on VM-B. If VM-B fails, the web application on VM-A cannot access data and effectively fails.

⚠️ What Causes Service Downtime?

Planned Downtime

  • Hardware installation & maintenance
  • Software upgrades or patches
  • Application or data restores

Unplanned Downtime

  • Human errors (accidental deletion, misconfiguration)
  • Database corruption
  • Component failures (hardware, software, network)
  • Disasters (fire, flooding, earthquakes, cyberattacks)

Impact of Service Unavailability

  • Direct Cost: Lost revenue, equipment rental, overtime pay
  • Reputation Damage: Customer loss, trust erosion
  • Real-world example: 2016 NHS hospital hack = 5 days downtime, 2,800 canceled appointments

🔧 4 Main Strategies to Achieve High Availability

1️⃣ Build Resilient Cloud Infrastructure

Goal: Avoid single points of failure (SPOF) - no one component can bring down the whole system.

A) Compute Level - Clustering

Clustering: 2+ servers work together as one system. If one fails, service automatically fails over to another.
  • Active/Active: Both servers actively serve requests
  • Active/Passive: One active, one standby (takes over if needed)

B) Network Level - Multiple Paths

  • Link Aggregation: Multiple network links → one logical link for redundancy
  • NIC Teaming: Groups multiple NICs, automatic failover if one fails
  • Switch Aggregation: Two physical switches appear as one; if one fails, traffic flows through the other
  • Multipathing: Multiple paths to storage; if one path fails, I/O automatically reroutes

C) Storage Level - RAID & Disk Sparing

  • RAID: Combines multiple disks into logical unit with data protection
  • Dynamic Disk Sparing: Failed disk automatically replaced by spare disk without manual intervention

D) N+1 Redundancy

N components + 1 standby = if one fails, you still have N working. Example: 3 servers + 1 spare = 4 total.

2️⃣ Automated Cloud Service Failover

Service Availability Zones (SAZ): Isolated locations with their own resources. Failure in one zone doesn't affect others.
  • Can be part of one data center or an entire data center
  • Multiple zones across different geographic locations survive data center failures
  • Enables automatic failover = service keeps running

3️⃣ Data Protection (Backup & Replication)

A) Backup

Backup: Additional copy of data created for disaster recovery. Must be restored by backup software before use.
  • Always a point-in-time copy (specific moment)
  • Limited recovery points (can only restore to last backup)
  • Slower RTO than replication (needs restoration process)

B) Replication

Replication: Exact copy of data immediately accessible by applications.
  • Local Replication: Copy within same location (snapshots, mirroring)
  • Remote Replication: Copy to different geographic location

Synchronous vs Asynchronous Replication

Type How It Works RPO Use Case
Synchronous Replica always identical to source in real-time Near-zero RPO Mission-critical data
Asynchronous Replica lags behind source by some time Higher RPO Less critical data, cost-saving

Backup vs Replication Comparison

Feature Backup Replication
Accessibility Must be restored first Immediately accessible
RTO Slower (includes restore time) Faster
Copy Type Point-in-time only Can be continuous
Purpose Operational/disaster recovery Recovery and restart

Tiered Data Protection Approach

Different services get different protection levels based on importance:
  • Tier 1: Mission-critical (banks, hospitals) → synchronized replication + backup
  • Tier 2: Important but less urgent → periodic backup
  • Tier 3: Non-critical → basic backup only

4️⃣ Resilient Cloud Applications

Applications designed to keep running even when components fail.
  • Loosely coupled modules: If one breaks, others still work
  • Retry logic: Automatically retries if a service is temporarily down
  • Limited functionality mode: App continues with reduced features instead of crashing
  • Example: If payment system is down, allow browsing but queue orders for later

⚡ Quick Reference Cards

SPOF (Single Point of Failure)

One component whose failure brings down entire system. BC eliminates these through redundancy.

Active/Active

All redundant components actively working and distributing load

Active/Passive

One component active, others standby. Standby takes over on failure

Failover

Automatic switching from failed component to backup

📌 Real-World Examples

❌ Example 1: NHS Hospital Attack (2016)

What Happened: Firewall misconfiguration allowed virus to infect hospital network

Impact:
  • 3 hospitals shut down for 5 days
  • 2,800+ patient appointments canceled
  • Patients turned away and redirected
Lesson: Officials KNEW about the firewall problem before the attack but didn't fix it in time!

✅ Example 2: Cantey Technology (2013)

What Happened: Lightning caused fire at office building housing IT company's infrastructure

The Disaster:
  • Servers melted, cables burned, entire office destroyed
  • Company hosted servers for 200+ clients
Why They Survived:
  • Had BC plan: servers replicated to remote data center
  • Continuous backups stored remotely
  • Moved staff to temporary office
Result: Clients experienced ZERO downtime! They never knew anything happened.

🎯 Exam Tips & Key Takeaways

Remember the 4 Pillars:
  1. Build Resilient Infrastructure (redundancy)
  2. Automated Failover (availability zones)
  3. Data Protection (backup + replication)
  4. Resilient Applications (fault-tolerant code)
Key Differences to Know:
  • Backup vs Replication - This is a common exam question
  • RPO vs RTO - Not the same thing! RPO = interval between backups, RTO = time to restore
  • Synchronous vs Asynchronous - Know the trade-offs
  • Local vs Remote Replication - Geography matters
Calculation Practice:
  • Know the availability formula: (Agreed Time - Downtime) / Agreed Time
  • Practice calculating percentages with different downtime scenarios
Common Misconceptions to Avoid:
  • BC is NOT just backup - it includes infrastructure redundancy too
  • Replication is NOT the same as backup
  • Active/Passive is NOT the same as Active/Active
